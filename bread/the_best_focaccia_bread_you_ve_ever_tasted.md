# The Best Focaccia Bread You've Ever Tasted

## Ingredients:

### Simple Ingredients

- 3.9 dl Water (400 milliliters)
- 4 tsp. Fresh yeast (10 g, or 2 tsp. (6 g) dry yeast)
- 1 tsp. Sugar (helps activate the yeast)
- 480 g Bread flour (500 g)
- 0.3 dl. Olive oil (plus extra for greasing and drizzling)
- 2 tsp. Salt (plus coarse salt for topping)

### Tips & Tricks

- Don't be shy with the olive oil - focaccia truly loves it and it creates that amazing crispy crust!
- Wet hands are your best friend when handling this sticky dough - it prevents sticking perfectly
- Those bubbles you see forming are excellent signs - they create the light, airy texture inside
- The 'lift and slap' technique is key - it develops the gluten structure without traditional kneading
- Don't worry if the dough doesn't cover the entire tray - give it time to relax and try again
- The dimpling should go almost to the bottom of the pan - don't be gentle, create those signature wells
- Listen for the 'singing' as it cools - those crackling sounds mean you've achieved the perfect crust

### Step-by-Step Instructions

1. 1 Dissolve the yeast: In a large bowl, mix water with Fresh yeast (or dry yeast) and sugar. Stir until the yeast is completely dissolved - this activates the yeast for a beautiful rise.
2. 2 Add the flour: Add all 480 g of bread flour to the water and yeast mixture. Mix with a wooden spoon until combined - the dough will be soft and easy to mix, no kneading needed!
3. 3 Add oil and salt: Pour in 0.3 dl of olive oil (because focaccia loooooves olive oil!) and 10 g of salt. Mix until everything is well incorporated.
4. 4 Clean and rest: That's it! No need to knead. Clean the spoon and scrape down the bowl sides. Cover and let rest for 30 minutes - the dough will start developing its structure naturally.
5. 5 Stretch and fold: Soak your hand in clean water (prevents sticking), then grab the 4 sides of the dough and fold them to the center. This stretching helps develop the gluten network perfectly.
6. 6 Lift and slap: Lift the dough from the center and slap it into the bowl a couple of times. Remember to soak your hand before each fold - this technique creates the perfect texture!
7. 7 Second rest: Cover and let rest for another 30 minutes. You'll notice the dough becoming more elastic and developing beautiful bubbles - it's already getting wobbly!
8. 8 Final stretch and fold: Repeat the same process - stretch and fold the 4 sides to the center, then lift and slap a couple of times. The dough should be full of bubbles and wonderfully wobbly now!
9. 9 Prepare the tray: Grease your baking tray generously with olive oil - be generous, focaccia loves olive oil! This creates that amazing crispy bottom crust.
10. 10 Transfer the dough: Carefully transfer the dough into the greased tray. Add more olive oil on top - yes, trust me on this! Give it a relaxing olive oil massage.
11. 11 Spread gently: The goal is to spread the dough toward the edges, but don't worry if it doesn't cover the entire tray perfectly - rustic is beautiful! Cover with another tray and let rest for 30 minutes.
12. 12 Preheat oven: Preheat your oven to 225°C (437°F) (230°C (446°F)). While it's heating, prepare for the final touches to your focaccia masterpiece.
13. 13 Create the dimples: here is the fun part! Add just a little more olive oil on top, then dimple the focaccia with your fingers. Dip your fingers until you feel the tray - don't be too gentle, create those signature focaccia dimples!
14. 14 Final touches: Sprinkle a little water on top (this helps create steam for a crispy crust), then sprinkle coarse salt generously over the surface.
15. 15 Bake to perfection: Bake at 225°C (437°F) (230°C (446°F)) for 20 minutes until golden brown and beautifully bubbly. The aroma will be absolutely incredible!
16. 16 Cool and enjoy: Transfer to a wire rack to cool (if you have the patience!). Listen to it sing as it cools - those little crackling sounds mean you've achieved focaccia perfection. The crust should be crispy and the inside light as a feather.

### Notes

17. • Bread flour can be substituted with all-purpose flour, but texture may vary
18. • Rising times may vary depending on room temperature and humidity
19. • Store finished bread in an airtight container for up to 3 days
20. • To restore the bread's quality, heat up a pan, put the bread in, splash a few drops of water directly onto the hot pan, and cover it with a lid for a few minutes
